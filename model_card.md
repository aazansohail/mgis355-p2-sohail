# Model card: startup exit screener

| Field | |
|---|---|
| **Decision and user** | A VC associate or accelerator screener decides whether to take a first meeting with a startup that just closed its first funding round. One decision per startup, dozens per week (the 2008 test year alone has 1,648 first rounds, about 35 a week). High score: take the meeting. Low score: pass for now. |
| **Unit of prediction** | One row per company, as of the date of its first recorded funding round. |
| **Target** | `exited = 1` if the company was acquired or went public on or before the date 5 years after its first funding round; otherwise 0. Exit date = earliest dated acquisition or IPO. **Excluded:** companies with no dated round; first rounds after 2008-12-12 (no full 5-year window before the snapshot ends 2013-12-12); first rounds before 2000-01-01 (placeholder and back-filled dates); exits dated before the first round; companies that did exit but on an unknown date (35); people, investors and products. |
| **Data** | Crunchbase 2013 Snapshot © 2013 (CC BY), Kaggle "Startup Investments". Real data. 8,214 companies, first rounds 2000-01-01 to 2008-12-12, outcomes observed through 2013-12-12. 1,185 exits (14.4%). Details in `data.md`. |
| **Features** | First-round amount in USD (known at prediction time? yes) · investors in the first round (yes) · company age at the first round (yes) · round stage: seed/angel, Series A, Series B+, venture unlabeled, debt, grant, other (yes) · industry category (yes) · country (yes) · US state (yes). Zeros and impossible dates are treated as missing, with a missing flag. |
| **Excluded on purpose** | `status` (it is the outcome: adding it gives test AUC 0.997) · `funding_rounds`, `funding_total_usd` (count rounds raised after the prediction date; AUC 0.676 and 0.681 when added, so they add nothing but risk) · first-round year (known, but every new case is outside the training years, and it encodes market drift) · IDs, names, URLs, descriptions (identifiers, not features) · investor identities (would need point-in-time investor track records; out of scope). |
| **Split** | Time split by first-round date. Train: 6,566 startups, 2000-01-01 to 2008-02-24, 15.2% exited. Test: 1,648 startups, 2008-02-25 to 2008-12-12, 11.5% exited. Settings and the threshold were chosen on a validation slice (the latest 1,314 training startups, 2007-08-15 to 2008-02-24). Why: the screener scores startups funded after the data the model learned from. A random split gives about the same AUC (0.670) but a 14.4% test base rate and a model that looks calibrated (14.2% predicted); the time split shows the real problem: on the next cohort the model over-predicts (13.6% predicted vs 11.5% actual). |
| **Baseline** | "Meet if the first round is Series A or later, or raised at least $500K" (cutoff chosen on train by cost). Test: 74 meetings per 100 startups, catches 156 of 189 exits (83%), $25,394 per 100 startups. "Predict no exit" is 88.5% accurate and costs $34,405 per 100. |
| **Model** | Logistic regression (C = 0.01; log amounts, median imputation with missing flags, one-hot categories). Test AUC **0.680**. At the 0.13 threshold: 51 meetings per 100, catches 142 of 189 exits (75%), **$21,371 per 100 startups, $4,023 (16%) less than the baseline**. At the baseline's own meeting count (1,221), its top-ranked startups include 167 exits vs the baseline's 156. A gradient-boosted model (test AUC 0.677, $21,845 per 100) had to beat it on both test cost and AUC to ship. It did not. |
| **Threshold and cost** | Wasted meeting (false positive) = $300: about 3 hours of associate time for prep, meeting and notes at about $100/hour (my assumption). Missed exit (false negative) = 10x = $3,000 (my checkpoint assumption: venture returns come from a few exits, so missing one is at least 10x worse). With calibrated probabilities that implies a 1/11 = 0.091 cutoff; the lowest-cost cutoff on validation was **0.13**, higher because the model over-predicts on later cohorts. Chosen on validation, applied to test unchanged. The default 0.5 cutoff would flag no startup at all (the highest test score is 0.35); the textbook 0.091 would take 79 meetings per 100 and cost $23,829 per 100. |
| **Where it is weakest** | Startups whose first round lists no investor count (19% of test startups): AUC 0.53 and the model flags none of them at 0.13, so it misses all 20 of their exits. Outside the US it catches 42% of exits (19 of 45) vs 85% in the US. |
| **Do not use it for** | Deciding whether or how much to invest: it predicts whether any exit happens, including small acquisitions, not the size of the return. Also not for startups funded after 2013 without retraining, or for companies already past their first round. |
| **What would change the recommendation** | The model is the cheapest rule only when a missed exit costs between 3 and 15 wasted meetings. At 20 or more, meeting everyone costs the same or less (1,459 vs 1,466 cost units on test); at 2, meeting no one is cheaper. If the screener can only take a fixed number of meetings, use the ranking (top N) instead of the threshold. If a recent cohort's exit rate moves far from 11.5%, recalibrate before trusting the 0.13 cutoff. |
